\section{Experimental Protocol}

\subsection{Chronological Evaluation and Leakage Controls}

All model development follows the chronological train-validation-test design in Table~\ref{tab:data-splits}. Training data are used for fitting parameters, scalers, and imputers. Validation data are used for neural selection, advanced GARCH-family selection, calibration fitting, refit protocol selection, and combination-weight selection. Test data are reserved for final evaluation.

The target, target date, date, and split labels are excluded from predictive features. Rolling features are trailing-only. LSTM validation and test sequences are built within each split so no sequence crosses a split boundary. Econometric parameters are estimated on training data unless an explicitly documented refit protocol is being evaluated.

\subsection{Metrics and Forecast Comparison}

For aligned observations \(\{y_t,\hat{y}_t\}_{t=1}^n\), the evaluation uses
\begin{align}
\mathrm{RMSE} &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}(y_t-\hat{y}_t)^2},\\
\mathrm{MAE} &= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}|y_t-\hat{y}_t|,\\
\mathrm{QLIKE} &= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\left(\log(\hat{y}_t)+\frac{y_t}{\hat{y}_t}\right).
\end{align}
Predicted variances are clipped at \(\epsilon=10^{-8}\) before QLIKE computation. QLIKE is central because severe underprediction is penalized through \(y_t/\hat{y}_t\). MAE is also reported, but it can be misleading for volatility forecasting when low forecasts perform well on many calm days yet fail during volatility spikes.

Diebold-Mariano tests use the loss-difference convention
\begin{equation}
d_t = L_{m,t}-L_{b,t},
\end{equation}
where \(L_{m,t}\) is the compared model loss and \(L_{b,t}\) is the benchmark loss. A positive mean difference means the compared model is worse than the benchmark. Multiple testing is summarized with Holm correction, and a documented moving-block bootstrap approximation is used for the MCS analysis.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Original Benchmark Results}

\input{tables/model_comparison_common_test}

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} reports the original common-window comparison. GARCH(1,1) has the lowest QLIKE, 1.052888, and the lowest RMSE, 3.648839. ARIMA-GARCH is very close, with QLIKE 1.055679, and the QLIKE Diebold-Mariano test against GARCH gives \(p=0.399560\). This is consistent with limited standalone value from ARIMA mean filtering for next-day variance forecasting.

The LSTM and ARIMA-GARCH-LSTM forecasts do not beat GARCH(1,1) by QLIKE. The log-target variants tell a different metric story: Hybrid-LogTarget-Small has the lowest MAE, 1.108165, but QLIKE rises to 2.232917 and mean predicted variance falls to 0.333670 against mean actual variance 1.211912. This is the clearest example of why MAE alone is not a reliable volatility-forecasting criterion.

\subsection{Econometric Diagnostics}

The diagnostic tests support conditional-variance modeling. The ADF test on percentage log returns strongly rejects a unit root with statistic \(-41.084280\) and p-value 0. Ljung-Box tests indicate dependence in returns and squared returns, and the ARCH-LM test on returns gives statistic 480.759753 with p-value \(5.69\times 10^{-97}\). The fitted GARCH models converged, and residual ARCH-LM p-values for standardized residuals are high after fitting.

The fitted GARCH(1,1) persistence \(\alpha+\beta\) is 0.973264. The ARIMA-GARCH residual-volatility leg has persistence 0.971886. These values indicate strong volatility persistence. The ARIMA-GARCH GARCH leg is fitted to zero-mean residuals, so its mean parameter is not available in the stored residual-GARCH artifact.

\subsection{Advanced GARCH-Family Search}

\input{tables/advanced_garch_selected_models}

Table~\ref{tab:advanced-garch} summarizes the selected advanced GARCH-family specifications. The validation-selected overall model, AdvGARCH-BestQLIKE, obtains validation QLIKE 1.611304 and full-window test QLIKE 1.032548. AdvGARCH-BestAsymmetric obtains the best standalone advanced test QLIKE, 1.029992, but that test-best statement is exploratory because the model is validation-selected within the asymmetric category rather than selected as the single overall model by test performance.

Raw QLIKE Diebold-Mariano tests show several advanced GARCH-family improvements against GARCH(1,1). AdvGARCH-BestAsymmetric has raw \(p=0.001534\), and AdvGARCH-BestQLIKE has raw \(p=0.018111\). Table~\ref{tab:stat-tests} shows why the interpretation remains cautious: no QLIKE improvement over GARCH(1,1) survives Holm correction at the 10\% level, and the approximate MCS retains 68 of 73 models at both 90\% and 95\%.

\input{tables/statistical_comparison_summary}

Refit protocols do not materially change the main conclusion. The best selected refit for the best GARCH-family model is fixed train-only. Original GARCH improves only slightly under expanding quarterly refit, with test QLIKE 1.059784 in the refit table, so rolling or expanding refitting is not supported as a robust improvement mechanism here.

\subsection{Neural Calibration and Forecast Combination}

\input{tables/calibration_combination_summary}

Table~\ref{tab:calibration-combinations} reports the calibration and combination evidence. Raw log-target neural forecasts have average test QLIKE 2.225569 and pred/actual ratio 0.273. Calibration improves the best calibrated log-target QLIKE to 1.146217 and changes the pred/actual ratio to 1.496. This supports scale bias as one cause of poor raw log-target QLIKE, but calibrated neural forecasts still do not beat the best advanced GARCH-family forecasts.

The best validation-selected combination is ComboStack-PoolD\_AllStable, with test QLIKE 1.113530. Its stacking weights are concentrated on calibrated log-target isotonic forecasts. The best observed test combination, ComboPair-BestGARCHFamily\_HybridQLIKE, has test QLIKE 1.026265 and weights 0.73 on AdvGARCH-BestARIMA and 0.27 on Hybrid-QLIKE. This suggests possible complementary information in hybrid signals, but it is not evidence of robust hybrid superiority because the validation-selected combination trails the best GARCH-family models and the best-test combination is exploratory.

\subsection{VaR Backtesting}

\input{tables/var_backtesting_summary}

Table~\ref{tab:var-backtests} shows that QLIKE performance and VaR coverage are related but not identical. At 5\% VaR, GARCH(1,1) and ARIMA-GARCH are closest to nominal, with 35 violations against 37.25 expected and Kupiec \(p=0.7025\). At 1\% VaR, Hybrid-QLIKE and the validation-selected combination each have 10 violations against 7.26 expected, with Kupiec \(p=0.3337\).

Raw LSTM-LogTarget is the main risk failure. It has 87 violations at 5\% and 56 violations at 1\%, far above the expected counts. This aligns with the QLIKE and underprediction evidence: low-MAE log-target forecasts can materially understate risk.

\subsection{Volatility Proxy Robustness}

\input{tables/proxy_robustness_rank_summary}

Proxy robustness changes exact rankings. The close-to-close squared-return proxy ranks ComboPair-BestGARCHFamily\_HybridQLIKE first by QLIKE. Parkinson, Garman-Klass, Rogers-Satchell, and Yang-Zhang 5-day proxies rank AdvGARCH-BestAsymmetric first. Yang-Zhang 10-day ranks GARCH(1,1) first, tied through ComboPair-GARCH11\_ARIMAGARCH, and Yang-Zhang 20-day ranks RollingVol-20 first.

Table~\ref{tab:proxy-robustness} summarizes rank stability. GARCH(1,1) remains competitive, with average QLIKE rank 7.00 and top-5 placement under 2 of 7 proxies, but it is not consistently top-tier under range-based proxies. AdvGARCH-BestAsymmetric is more stable, with average rank 3.29 and top-3 placement under 5 of 7 proxies. Raw log-target models remain problematic under proxy robustness: their average pred/actual ratio is approximately 0.356 and average spike-underprediction rate is 1.000.

Proxy-specific Holm tests reinforce the cautious interpretation. AdvGARCH-BestAsymmetric has Holm-significant QLIKE improvements over GARCH(1,1) for the close-to-close proxy and for Garman-Klass and Rogers-Satchell; several advanced GARCH variants are Holm-significant under Parkinson. No Yang-Zhang proxy shows a Holm-significant improvement over GARCH(1,1). The stable conclusion is therefore not that one model wins under every proxy, but that advanced asymmetric GARCH-family specifications are the most robust top performers.
